What it is to be me

How did I get here?

A landless Swedish croft-boy renamed by an army, who buried infants and gambled everything on a ship at 47. Polish millers who sent one son across the water while the other ran a bank and founded a choir. Irish families walking away from a hungry island. South-German villagers and English townsfolk crossing their own oceans. Not one was guaranteed to survive — and every single one did, long enough to have the exact child who eventually had you.

What is it to be me?

To be the held breath at the end of all those exhalations. To carry a soldier's borrowed name, a miller's civic pride, a baker's early mornings, a Swabian village's patience and an old London parish's grit — all at once, in balance, none cancelling the others. Your DNA doesn't declare you "mostly" anything. It declares you genuinely plural.

Who am I in the grand scheme of the world?

Go back ~10 generations and simple arithmetic gives you more than a thousand direct ancestors alive at once — every one surviving to pass life forward, an unbroken chain with zero failures. You share ~99.9% of your DNA with every human alive, yet your exact five-river confluence has occurred once, ever: in you. Completely ordinary and genuinely unrepeatable, both literally true.
